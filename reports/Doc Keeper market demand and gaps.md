# Doc Keeper Targets Real but Narrow Demand

The need Doc Keeper serves is real, but the market for it is modest and crowded. Distribution is now a bigger threat than lack of demand. The leading dedicated ID and document wallet, Folio, has reached about **500K+ Google Play installs and about 7K US App Store ratings after roughly nine years**. Most rivals sit in the 10K–100K band, and more than ten 2026 entrants have almost no ratings. Doc Keeper itself has no measurable user base: it is not on Google Play, and none of the research found install or usage data for it. Demand is strongest not for "photos of my ID", which Google Wallet, Apple Wallet, DigiLocker and UAE PASS now handle for free, but for the jobs those wallets leave undone:

- documents from many issuers and other countries
- family members' documents
- insurance, certificates and contracts
- staged expiry warnings before costly deadlines
- surviving a phone change without losing everything

User reviews of the category leader most often complain about data loss after updates or phone switches, followed by poor scanning, opaque cloud storage, and (in general document apps) subscription resentment. Doc Keeper's offline encryption, OCR search, persons and one-time price fit those pains well. Several competitors offer the same "offline + encrypted + reminders + family" combination, though. Doc Keeper also faces Google's developer-verification rules, which from 2027 will put a 24-hour developer-mode wait in front of every new install of an unregistered sideloaded app. Its edge therefore has to come from specific gaps: Arabic-aware capture, expiry auto-extraction, renewal workflows, and backup that cannot fail silently. It also has to come from fixing distribution first.

## A 500K-install ceiling defines the dedicated category

Store data puts hard numbers on the category. On Google Play, the closest competitors are Folio (**500K+ installs, 4.6 stars, 6.6K reviews**) ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.folioltd&hl=en_US)), "Wallet - Digital Cards & IDs" (500K+) ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.walletapp.digitalcards.ids&hl=en_US)), SUNMARE's "Wallet - Digital Wallet Card" (1M+, 20.5K reviews) ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cards.wallet.cardsapp&hl=en_US)), MyDocs (100K+) ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=work.opale.mydocs&hl=en_US)) and Crunch's ID Card Wallet (100K+, $3.49 one-time unlock) ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.crunch.idcardwallet&hl=en_US)). On iOS, Folio has **6,950 US ratings** and has been available since 2017 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1266382717)). Folio says it holds "over a million documents" for users in more than 120 countries ([Folio](https://folio.id/en/about/)). The most successful document vaults therefore end up with hundreds of thousands of users after many years, not millions.

Adjacent categories put that ceiling in context. Loyalty-card wallets are about ten times larger: Cards - Mobile Wallet has 10M+ installs and PassWallet 5M+ ([Cards](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cardsapp.android&hl=en_US), [PassWallet](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.attidomobile.passwallet&hl=en_US)). Password managers that also store files are larger again: Keeper 10M+, Bitwarden 5M+, 1Password 1M+ ([Keeper](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.callpod.android_apps.keeper&hl=en_US), [Bitwarden](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.x8bit.bitwarden&hl=en_US), [1Password](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.onepassword.android&hl=en_US)). Google Wallet has 1B+ installs ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.walletnfcrel&hl=en_US)). People want cards on their phone far more often than documents on their phone. A documents-first app is aiming at the smaller pool.

Two further signals temper the picture. First, **every standalone expiry-reminder app checked on Play has fewer than 1K installs** (DocuAlert has 500+) ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.docualert.documents_expiry_reminder&hl=en_US)). Reminders are a feature people value inside a vault, not a product on their own. Second, the long tail keeps growing. At least ten document-wallet or expiry apps released on iOS between January and October 2026 have 0–3 US ratings, including VaultKeep, Expiro and Renewo ([iTunes search](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=document+expiry&entity=software&country=us)). Play searches for "document wallet" or "document expiry reminder" each return 13–31 apps, many of them new ([Google Play search](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=document+wallet&c=apps)). Developers clearly believe there is demand, but new entrants are not winning users. Discovery, not the existence of demand, is the binding constraint.

Analyst figures, such as **USD 11.6B for "digital ID wallets" in 2024** ([GII/360iResearch](https://www.gii.co.jp/report/ires1718462-digital-id-wallet-market-by-technology-type-device.html)) and **USD 2.5–4.2B for password management** in 2025 with growth estimates that disagree widely ([MarketResearch.com](https://www.marketresearch.com/Deep-Insights-Research-Co-DIR-v4285/Global-Password-Management-Research-Competitive-41704005), [Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/password-management-Industry-103753)), mostly measure government and enterprise identity spending. They say little about a paid, offline consumer vault. Search-volume data for terms like "document wallet app" or "passport expiry reminder" was not available, so keyword demand is still unknown. There are also no public revenue estimates for any dedicated document-wallet app.

## Government and platform wallets absorb the simple ID case

The biggest change in the landscape is that operating systems and governments now hold the user's primary ID for free. **DigiLocker reached 72.43 crore (about 724M) registered users by July 2026** ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/digilocker-crosses-7243-crore-users-umang-records-1166-crore-registrations-govt-informs-rajya-sabha)). **UAE PASS passed 12M users**, up from 7.2M in 2023 ([WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/c1betiw-world-economic-forum-recognises-uae-pass-global)). Apple launched a passport-derived Digital ID in November 2025 ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/11/apple-introduces-digital-id-a-new-way-to-create-and-present-an-id-in-apple-wallet/)), and state driver's licences reached a 14th state in May 2026 ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/05/27/apple-wallet-ids-14th-state/)). Most directly relevant for an Android app, Google Wallet's "Everything Else" pass lets any user photograph an ID or insurance card. AI reads the fields, and government IDs stay on the device behind authentication ([Google Wallet Help](https://support.google.com/wallet/answer/12060038?hl=en)). It launched in the US in August 2024 and was rolling out internationally by September 2025 ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-wallet-everything-else-pass-rolling-out-3593890/), [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/08/12/google-wallet-everything-else/)). For "show my card at the counter", a paid app that only stores photos is now competing with a free app that comes preinstalled.

Each of these wallets is limited to its own jurisdiction or its own format, though. UAE PASS holds documents issued by UAE departments, such as the Emirates ID, residence visa and driving licence ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/telephone-internet/uae-residents-tired-of-looking-for-documents-keep-everything-handy-on-your-phone-1.1720101299275)). Visitors who only have a passport cannot use the vault until they verify with an Emirates ID ([Wego](https://blog.wego.com/uae-pass/)). Apple's Digital ID works for TSA checks on domestic US flights only and "does not replace a physical passport" ([MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2025/11/12/apple-launches-digital-id-passport-feature)). Google says its ID pass "isn't a government-issued ID or a replacement for your physical ID" ([Google Wallet Help](https://support.google.com/wallet/answer/15284332)). The EU's EUDI Wallet is due by 24 December 2026, but **24 of 27 member states were expected to miss that deadline** ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/16/eu-digital-wallet-24-of-27-members-will-miss-the-deadline)). Official wallets are also unreliable. UAE PASS reviewers describe OTP failures, crashes at launch and endless "scan Emirates ID" screens ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/uae-pass/id1377158818)), and Apple Community threads report failed passport enrolments ([Apple Community](https://origin-discussions2-us-dr-prz.apple.com/thread/256187116)).

The opportunity that remains is the **"everything the government wallet does not hold"** vault. It covers an expatriate's home-country passport and tax papers, private health and car insurance, children's birth and school certificates issued abroad, tenancy and employment contracts, degrees, and warranties. Google Wallet's photo pass also does not offer multi-page PDFs, full-text search across documents, per-person organisation or use without an account. No source confirmed whether it sends expiry reminders for photo passes. Government wallets are probably helping Doc Keeper's category more than hurting it, because they normalise "my documents live on my phone". No study has measured this either way, however.

## Data loss, not missing features, drives the angriest reviews

The user-pain evidence is a small sample of about 20 review quotes, mostly about Folio, so it points to which pains exist rather than measuring how common they are. Within it, one theme dominates: **documents vanishing after an update, a reinstall or a phone change**. Folio reviewers write that "all of those cards that I took the time to enter… is gone", that photos "turned into gray rectangles" and "I couldn't get the back up to work", and that after switching iPhones "all my Folio content was gone". The developer blamed a forgotten restore password ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1266382717/folio-digital-wallet-app/reviews), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/folio-digital-wallet-app/id1266382717?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)). Android users report the same thing ("everything that used to be in my wallet just vanished") ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/folio-digital-wallet-app/com.folioltd)). Close behind is the complaint that export and migration are "extremely difficult" and that the app gives "no information whatsoever about backing up or restoring" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/folio-digital-wallet-app/id1266382717?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)), along with a 2024 Trustpilot review asking whether a PDF export is even possible ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/folio.id?page=3)). The Folio case shows that encryption itself can cause data loss when the restore password is lost. That warning applies directly to Doc Keeper's password-protected backup file.

The other pains form a clear second tier. Capture quality frustrates users: Folio's auto-detection is "practically worthless", images come out "very blue", and steadying the phone for a decent scan is a chore ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1266382717/folio-digital-wallet-app/reviews)). Some users find out only later that their data is stored in the cloud ("I realize Folio saves all your cards online, not in your phone") and ask whether "my data is the product" (same source). Subscription anger shows up mostly in general document apps rather than ID wallets. Readdle Documents users object to paying "$50 or $90" and say core features "should not be something locked behind a subscription service" ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/364901807/documents-file-manager-docs/reviews)). Doc Keeper's pitch of a one-time purchase and no cloud answers these complaints. Folio's overall 4.7-star rating is a reminder that most users are satisfied, and that these complaints come from a vocal minority who lost something they cared about ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/folio-digital-wallet-app/id1266382717)).

Outside app reviews, people mostly improvise. Immigrants scan documents into Google Drive and keep a USB copy at home ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/immigrants-where-do-you-store-your-immigration-documents-qwr6q5pb)), and technical users put PDFs in a password manager ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=29173657)). These setups give no expiry tracking, no per-family-member structure and no fast lookup. Those gaps cost real money:

| Missed deadline | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Late Emirates ID renewal | **AED 20 per day, capped at AED 1,000** ([UAE Explained](https://uaeexplained.com/emirates-id-fine/)); banks may freeze accounts and telecoms suspend SIMs ([Just Dubai](https://www.just-dubai.com/article/emirates-id-expiring-soon-what-should-you-do-first/8127)) |
| UAE residence visa lapses | The Emirates ID expires with it; a 30-day renewal window applies ([u.ae](https://u.ae/en/archived-content/residence-visa/renewing-your-residence-visa)) |
| Passport within the six-month validity rule | Airlines deny boarding even before the passport technically expires ([VFS Global](https://www.vfsglobal.com/en/individuals/insights/six-month-validity-rule-explained.html)) |
| Passport renewed too late in a backlog | The 2023 US backlog reached 13 weeks and derailed trips ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2023/07/08/1186372900/canceled-trips-and-no-refunds-passport-delays-are-derailing-travelers)) |

Lost documents are common too. About **8% of Britons have lost a passport abroad** ([Confused.com](https://www.confused.com/press/releases/2024/more-than-83000-uk-passports-lost-or-stolen-abroad-in-5-years)), as have 7% of Americans ([YouGov](https://today.yougov.com/travel/articles/46028-adults-under-30-more-likely-have-us-passport)). Japan recorded about 28,000 lost or stolen passports in 2025 ([MOFA](https://www.mofa.go.jp/press/release/pressite_000001_02099.html)). A secure copy matters most at exactly those moments. Because of the validity rules, a reminder on the expiry date or 14 days before it is too late for passports. Useful warnings have to come months ahead.

One limit on this evidence: Reddit and Google Play review pages could not be reached, so Android-specific complaints and everyday frustrations like "fumbling for a document at a counter" are under-represented rather than absent.

## Offline vaults are table stakes; specific gaps remain open

Doc Keeper is not alone in its positioning. **Travel Document Vault** runs on iOS and Android, needs no account, encrypts with AES-256-GCM on the device, reads the passport MRZ on-device, offers family profiles and sells a "pay once" Pro tier ([Travel Document Vault](https://traveldocumentvault.com/features/)). **Proof Pocket** is offline, encrypts every file on the device, alerts at 90/30/7/1 days, organises by person and charges $39.99 lifetime or $14.99 a year ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/proof-pocket/id6753912757)). Vault Guardian is cloud-based, with 3 free documents and then $6.99–$10.99 a month ([Vault Guardian](https://vaultguardian.app/)). DocuPal already offers local storage with local reminders on Android ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tycomaclabs.docupalapp&hl=en)). Password managers add encrypted attachments for **$19.80–$71.88 a year** (for example Bitwarden Premium at $19.80) ([Bitwarden](https://bitwarden.com/pricing.md)). On-device encryption, a biometric lock, a scanner, expiry reminders and some form of export are therefore the minimum any vault needs, not differentiators.

Measured against that field, Doc Keeper has real but narrow advantages. It is the only surveyed vault with **full-text on-device OCR search across every document**; competitors' OCR is limited to MRZ or expiry-date extraction ([Travel Document Vault](https://traveldocumentvault.com/features/)). It adds spaces, tags and linked notes, and it covers contracts and certificates as well as cards. Its 10-document free tier is generous next to Vault Guardian's 3. Its one-time price undercuts both subscriptions and Proof Pocket's $39.99. It falls behind in six ways:

- It is not on Google Play.
- It has no iOS version, which weakens family use in mixed-platform households.
- It does not extract expiry dates from scans automatically.
- It uses a single 14-day reminder default, where rivals stage alerts at 90/30/7 days or 8 months for passports.
- It cannot back up to the user's own cloud.
- It has no family vault shared across devices.

The gaps that no surveyed app fills well, ranked by evidence and fit, are:

1. **Arabic-aware capture.** Google's on-device ML Kit text recognition supports Latin, Chinese, Devanagari, Japanese and Korean scripts but not Arabic ([Google ML Kit](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/text-recognition/v2)). Android vaults built on it cannot search the Arabic text on Emirates IDs, Iqamas, Ejari contracts or Gulf certificates. The accuracy of alternatives such as Tesseract's Arabic model on ID cards is unverified.
2. **Renewal workflows rather than bare reminders.** The UAE links residence visa, Emirates ID and dependants' visas: when the visa expires, the ID expires too ([u.ae](https://u.ae/en/archived-content/residence-visa/renewing-your-residence-visa)). No surveyed app models these dependencies, grace periods or daily fines.
3. **Automatic expiry extraction.** The passport MRZ is machine-readable Latin text, so this is feasible with the OCR Doc Keeper already has.
4. **Family sharing without a cloud.** Surveyed family features either depend on a cloud or work on one device only.
5. **Backup that cannot fail silently.** This needs nudges, tested restore and clear warnings about the password, given that Folio's worst reviews come from failed restores.

The best-fit segment is **Gulf expatriates**. The UAE had about 12.06M residents in 2025 ([Gulf Today](https://www.gulftoday.ae/news/2026/11/15/uae-population-crosses-12m-in-2025-records-7-increase-from-2024)), of whom about 88–89% are foreign nationals ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriates_in_the_United_Arab_Emirates)). The GCC hosts about 30M foreign nationals ([GLMM](https://gulfmigration.grc.net/about/context/)). These households hold interdependent, multi-issuer documents with fines attached, and UAE PASS and DigiLocker each cover only one side of them. Gulf News even runs explainers for "UAE residents tired of looking for documents" ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/telephone-internet/uae-residents-tired-of-looking-for-documents-keep-everything-handy-on-your-phone-1.1720101299275)). Secondary segments are frequent travellers and digital nomads (18.5M US workers in 2025) ([MBO Partners](https://mbopartners.com/state-of-independence/digital-nomads)) and families managing dependants' documents. Refugees (41.6M worldwide) ([UNHCR](https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/global-trends-report-2025.pdf)) have acute need but are better reached through NGOs than through a paid, sideloaded app. No source gave the Android share among Gulf expatriates, so the size of the segment Doc Keeper can actually reach is still an estimate.

## Sideloading-only distribution becomes a wall in 2027

The research also identifies Doc Keeper's own channel as its largest risk. Under Android developer verification, from **30 September 2026 apps must be registered by verified developers to install on certified devices in initial regions, with all regions following in 2027** ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/android-developer-verification-rolling-out-to-all-developers.html)). Unregistered apps will still install over ADB or through an "advanced flow". That flow means turning on Developer Mode, allowing unverified packages, restarting and **waiting 24 hours** ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/18/google-gradually-rolling-out-androids-advanced-sideloading-ahead-of-developer-verification/)). Mainstream users, Gulf expatriates included, will not do that. Developers can register apps they distribute outside Play through Play Console, which keeps a GitHub APK viable ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/android-developer-verification-rolling-out-to-all-developers.html)). A Play listing for a new personal account first requires a closed test with **12 testers opted in for 14 continuous days** ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en)).

Trust is harder still. A sideloaded app asks users to click past security warnings in order to protect their most sensitive documents. Privacy-first apps have built trust through open source code, independent audits and community listings, as Standard Notes did before joining Proton ([Proton](https://proton.me/blog/da/proton-standard-notes-join-forces), [Privacy Guides](https://www.privacyguides.org/software/notebooks/)). Claiming to be "offline" proves little on its own, because several Play listings already lead with "100% offline" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.crunch.idcardwallet&hl=en_US)). A claim users can check, such as no INTERNET permission in the manifest, reproducible builds or a published security write-up, carries more weight. The one-time price fits the market: small offline utilities charge $2.99–$3.49 once, and Proof Pocket's $39.99 lifetime tier sets the upper bound. Even so, a category whose best apps reach hundreds of thousands of installs over many years points to side-project-scale revenue unless distribution is unusually strong. No conversion-rate or revenue data for comparable apps was found to firm that up.

## Conclusion

The question is less whether demand exists than which demand Doc Keeper can still capture. Platform and government wallets have taken the simplest case, holding your own primary ID. The demand left over is defined by complexity: several countries, several family members, several issuers, deadlines that depend on each other and carry fines, and the need to survive a phone change. That points Doc Keeper away from being a general "ID wallet" and towards a household renewal manager for cross-border families. Gulf expatriates are the sharpest version of that customer, and Arabic-aware capture plus visa-to-ID dependency logic are things a feature-matched rival cannot easily copy. Doc Keeper's full-text OCR is a strong base for this, but only if it is extended to extract expiry dates and handle Arabic.

The competitors' review history points to an equally clear risk. In an offline-only app, the user carries the backup burden. If Doc Keeper does not prompt for backups, test restores and protect against forgotten passwords, it will collect the same "lost everything when I changed phones" reviews that dent Folio. Order matters too. Developer verification and a Play listing (or a verified alternative store) have to come before marketing spend, because by 2027 an unregistered APK will lose most of its potential users at the install screen. Doc Keeper's own usage and the size of its addressable segment are the two biggest unknowns. Both could be measured cheaply through a Play closed test aimed at expatriate community groups before investing further.
