# Doc Keeper: Underserved Segments, Regional Opportunities, Gaps and Distribution Barriers

Research date: 21 November 2026. Data years are stated next to each figure. Fourteen tool calls were used, mostly web searches. Some figures come from search-result summaries and secondary aggregators rather than from the primary page; these are flagged.

## 1. Which segments have the most acute need, and how large are they?

### Takeaway
The strongest segment, and the one closest to hand, is Gulf expatriates. In the UAE, about 88–89% of roughly 12 million residents are foreign nationals. Across the GCC there are about 30 million foreign nationals. Their documents tie together: when a residence visa lapses, the Emirates ID lapses with it. Renewals have short windows and daily fines. They also carry home-country documents that government apps do not hold. The second tier is families managing dependants' documents, frequent travellers and digital nomads (18.5M in the US alone), and refugees (41.6M worldwide). Refugees have acute need, but they are hard to reach with a paid, sideloaded Android app.

### Cited Findings
**Gulf expatriates and migrant workers**
- The UAE population reached 12.06 million in 2025, up 7% (about 770,000) from 11.3 million in 2024. This is FCSC data as reported by Gulf Today (Nov 2026). The population is 64% male, which fits a labour-migrant population — [Gulf Today](https://www.gulftoday.ae/news/2026/11/15/uae-population-crosses-12m-in-2025-records-7-increase-from-2024); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/uae-population-crosses-12-million-how-many-are-men-and-how-many-women-1.500748187)
- Expatriates are about 88–89% of the UAE population. Estimates range from 9.66M to 10.04M foreigners against about 1.31M Emiratis (mid-2025). These come from secondary aggregators; I did not see an official FCSC nationality split — [Wikipedia: Expatriates in the UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriates_in_the_United_Arab_Emirates); [GMI UAE population stats](https://www.globalmediainsight.com/blog/uae-population-statistics/)
- Dubai's expatriate share in 2025 was 92.02%. This is Dubai Statistics Center data as cited by GMI — [GMI Dubai population](https://www.globalmediainsight.com/blog/dubai-population-statistics/)
- The GCC hosts about 30 million foreign nationals, about 52% of the total population. The share ranges from 39% in Saudi Arabia to 88% in Qatar (mid-2020 data) — [GLMM, Gulf Research Center](https://gulfmigration.grc.net/about/context/)
- The GCC's 30M foreign nationals were about 11% of all international migrants in 2020. The number of foreign labourers fell by nearly one fifth between 2015 and 2021 — [Migration Policy Institute](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/article/gulf-region-gcc-migration-kafala-reforms)
- Renewal pressure in the UAE: sponsored residence visas run for 1, 2 or 3 years, and unsponsored ones up to 5 or 10 years — [u.ae residence visa provisions](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/visa-information/general-provisions-for-the-residence-visa). There is a 30-day window to renew after expiry, and "when the visa expires, your Emirates ID … also expires" — [u.ae (archived) renewing your residence visa](https://u.ae/en/archived-content/residence-visa/renewing-your-residence-visa)
- Fines for late renewal are reported inconsistently. Gulf News says AED 20/day for a late Emirates ID, capped at AED 1,000. Emirates247 says AED 50/day. PropertyFinder says AED 25–50/day, rising to about AED 100/day — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/uae-visa-emirates-id-know-the-updates-on-various-violations-and-fines-1.103489199); [Emirates247](https://www.emirates247.com/uae-guide/uae-residence-visa-renewal-guide-documents-steps-and-fees-explained/1942); [PropertyFinder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/blog/uae-visa-renewal/)
- Gulf News has published a consumer piece aimed at "UAE residents tired of looking for documents", on keeping everything on the phone. This is evidence of felt demand — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/telephone-internet/uae-residents-tired-of-looking-for-documents-keep-everything-handy-on-your-phone-1.1720101299275)

**Digital nomads and frequent travellers**
- 18.5 million American workers identified as digital nomads in 2025, up 2.2% year on year and about 12% of the US workforce. Of these, 11.2M hold traditional jobs, up 10%. This is MBO Partners 2025 State of Independence via search summary; MBO's policy page says 18.4M — [MBO Partners Digital Nomads](https://mbopartners.com/state-of-independence/digital-nomads); [MBO corporate nomad policy](https://www.mbopartners.com/state-of-independence/corporate-digital-nomad-policy)
- Global estimates of "40–80 million digital nomads" (2025) come from aggregator sites with unclear methods. Treat them as low-confidence — [Pumble](https://pumble.com/learn/digital-nomad-visa/statistics/)

**Refugees and displaced people**
- There were 117.8M forcibly displaced people at end-2025, down from 123.2M in 2024. Of these, 41.6M were refugees, 9M were asylum-seekers and 68.7M were internally displaced (UNHCR Global Trends, June 2026). The provisional mid-2026 refugee figure is 41.7M — [UNHCR Global Trends 2025 report](https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/global-trends-report-2025.pdf); [UNHCR Mid-Year Trends](https://www.unhcr.org/europe/mid-year-trends)

**Families managing dependants' documents**
- Saudi Arabia already lets expat sponsors view their dependants' digital Iqamas in Absher or Tawakkalna. This shows that household-level document management is an established expectation in the Gulf, though the source is a secondary guide — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/relocate/new-in-saudi-arabia-heres-how-you-can-carry-your-iqama-on-your-phone-1.1724162132574)
- Competing family-oriented vault apps on the app stores sell family tiers. Examples are "Document Expiry Reminder" (iOS, Family tier $69.99), "DocHarbor" ("your family's passports, IDs, licenses") and "Travel Document Vault" (family profiles). Demand is being met at least partly on iOS — [Document Expiry Reminder](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6756968613); [DocHarbor](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6786043565); [Travel Document Vault](https://www.producthunt.com/products/travel-document-vault)

### Inferences
- A Gulf expatriate typically has a UAE or GCC residence permit, a national ID, a driving licence, a home-country passport, home-country ID or tax documents, children's birth and school certificates, private health insurance and a tenancy contract. These documents have different issuers and expiry dates, and they depend on one another. A visa lapse cascades to the Emirates ID and onward. This is exactly where an offline vault with expiry tracking adds value.
- The UAE alone has about 10M expatriates. Even a niche share of Android-owning expatriate households would be meaningful for a one-time-purchase indie app. I found no source for Android market share among Gulf expats; see Gaps.
- Within the Gulf, South Asian migrant workers (Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Filipino, Nepali) are likely the largest group. They are probably Android-heavy and price-sensitive, which favours a one-time purchase. They are also linked to DigiLocker (India) for home documents. This is an inference and was not verified with a source.
- Refugees have acute need but low ability to pay, and they may lack stable devices. Sideloading friction would hit them hardest. This segment is better reached through NGO partnerships than through retail.
- Elderly people, caregivers and small landlords/freelancers are plausible segments, but I found no sizing data specific to document management for them.

### Gaps
- I found no official FCSC breakdown of UAE nationals versus expatriates, or of nationality mix. The 88–89% figure comes from aggregators.
- No source gave Android versus iOS share among UAE or GCC expatriates.
- I did not retrieve UN DESA International Migrant Stock 2024. The commonly cited figure of about 304M migrants worldwide is unverified here.
- I found no data sizing elderly or caregiver, small landlord or freelancer segments for document-management needs.
- I found no current GLMM total newer than about 2020–2022.

## 2. How do government wallets (UAE Pass, DigiLocker, Absher/Nafath, EUDI Wallet, Apple/Google Wallet IDs) affect the need, and what do they not cover?

### Takeaway
Government wallets are strong but limited to their own jurisdiction. Each one holds documents issued by its own government, and only for eligible residents. Expatriates fall between systems: UAE Pass holds the Emirates ID and visa but not the expatriate's foreign passport or home documents, and DigiLocker holds Indian documents but not UAE ones. None of them covers private insurance, warranties, receipts, school records or cross-border family documents in a single place. Apple and Google passport IDs are domestic-use credentials in a handful of countries, and Apple's are US-only. The EU wallet is late in most member states. These wallets reduce the need for photo copies of the citizen's own national ID, but they leave the cross-jurisdiction aggregation job unsolved.

### Cited Findings
**UAE Pass (UAE)**
- The UAE Pass vault lets users request digital versions of official documents and share them with service providers. It is blockchain-backed — [u.ae official portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/digital-uae/digital-transformation/platforms-and-apps/the-uae-pass-app)
- The vault stores documents issued by UAE government departments, for example the driving licence, Emirates ID and residence visa. These are added by choosing the issuing authority, such as ICP — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/telephone-internet/uae-residents-tired-of-looking-for-documents-keep-everything-handy-on-your-phone-1.1720101299275)
- In 2019 the Central Bank accepted vault documents as equivalent to physical copies for banks — [OECD OPSI](https://oecd-opsi.org/?p=34693)
- Visitors with only a passport get a basic account and cannot use the digital vault until they upgrade to a verified account, which usually needs an Emirates ID. This is a secondary source — [Wego](https://blog.wego.com/uae-pass/)
- Coverage is described as UAE-issued documents only; I found no evidence that foreign passports can be stored. UAE Pass reportedly provides access to about 5,000 government and private services — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/how-to-use-uae-pass-to-access-5000-government-and-private-services-in-the-uae-1.500168233)

**DigiLocker (India)**
- DigiLocker had 51.52 crore users (about 515M) and 943.36 crore documents issued (about 9.4B) as of March 2025 — [IMPRI policy brief](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/digilocker/)
- In August 2026 the government told the Rajya Sabha that DigiLocker had more than 72.43 crore registered users (about 724M) — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/digilocker-crosses-7243-crore-users-umang-records-1166-crore-registrations-govt-informs-rajya-sabha)
- Other figures conflict, such as "855.51 crore documents" undated on the Digital India homepage. Definitions vary — [Digital India](https://digitalindia.gov.in/)

**Saudi Arabia (Absher / Tawakkalna)**
- Expatriates can view their digital Iqama (residence ID) in Absher's "view digital documents" or in Tawakkalna, including for dependants — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/relocate/new-in-saudi-arabia-heres-how-you-can-carry-your-iqama-on-your-phone-1.1724162132574); [Arab News](https://arabnews.com/node/1825041)
- Expatriates can update passport details through Absher, which records the passport but does not act as a passport vault — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-expats-can-now-update-passport-details-via-absher-app-1.500089250)
- SAMA's rulebook accepts digital ID from Absher and Tawakkalna for banking — [SAMA rulebook](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/node/3365)
- I found no official page for Nafath's document scope; Nafath is primarily an authentication service.

**EU Digital Identity Wallet**
- Member states must make at least one EUDI Wallet available by 24 December 2026. Regulated entities must accept it about a year later — [Corbado](https://www.corbado.com/blog/eudi-wallet-2026-deadline-rollout-eic-2026); [Garrigues](https://www.garrigues.com/en_GB/garrigues-digital/european-digital-identity-wallet-now-has-timetable-tasks-be-completed-december)
- As of September 2026, Euronews reports that 24 of 27 member states had not made a wallet available and were expected to miss the deadline — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/16/eu-digital-wallet-24-of-27-members-will-miss-the-deadline); [Brussels Signal](https://brusselssignal.eu/2026/09/eu-digital-identity-wallet-stalls-in-24-member-states-as-brussels-reopens-its-privacy-rulebook/)
- Garrigues frames the obligation differently, as applying from when each wallet becomes available. This conflicts with the hard-deadline framing — [Garrigues](https://www.garrigues.com/en_GB/garrigues-digital/european-digital-identity-wallet-now-has-timetable-tasks-be-completed-december)

**Apple / Google Wallet IDs**
- Google Wallet launched passport-based digital IDs for the US, then expanded to the UK in 2025, then to Singapore, Taiwan and Brazil (with India also reported). An EU first wave (Spain, Italy, France, Ireland, Estonia) was planned for summer 2026, but I could not confirm it launched — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/google-pay/google-wallet-age-identity-verifications/); [ID Tech Wire](https://idtechwire.com/google-wallet-expands-digital-id-support-to-india-singapore-taiwan-and-brazil/); [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/04/30/google-wallet-expands-digital-passport-feature-to-more-countries/amp/)
- Apple's Digital ID from a US passport went live in November 2025. Apple says it does not replace a physical passport and cannot be used for international travel. Its uses are TSA checks at 250+ US airports and age verification — [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2025/11/12/apple-launches-digital-id-passport-feature)

### Inferences
- **Documents not covered by any of these wallets, and therefore Doc Keeper's addressable job:** foreign or home-country passports held while resident abroad; the home-country national ID, tax and property documents of an expatriate (outside the host wallet); private health, car and home insurance policies; warranties and receipts; children's birth, vaccination and school certificates from another country; tenancy contracts (Ejari has its own channel); employment contracts and labour cards from previous employers; professional certificates and degrees (attested copies); and pet and vehicle documents from abroad.
- Government wallets reinforce the habit of carrying documents on the phone, which lowers resistance to the idea. Doc Keeper should position as the "everything else" and cross-border vault next to UAE Pass and DigiLocker, not as a competitor to them.
- Government wallets usually need connectivity and an active residency. An offline vault is useful at borders, during visa gaps, after leaving a country (when access to the host wallet may lapse), and in poor-connectivity situations. This is an inference; I found no source confirming that UAE Pass vault access ends when someone leaves.

### Gaps
- No official UAE Pass page listing the full catalogue of vault documents, or confirming whether foreign passports are excluded.
- No official Nafath document scope.
- No confirmation of what happens to UAE Pass or Absher access after an expatriate exits the country.
- No confirmed live status for Google Wallet's EU ID rollout as of Nov 2026.

## 3. What gaps does no current app fill well?

### Takeaway
The market has many small iOS-first vault and expiry-reminder apps. None of those I found combines offline-only encryption, Arabic OCR, family profiles, cross-platform sharing without a cloud, and renewal guidance. Arabic OCR is a hard technical gap, because Google's on-device ML Kit Text Recognition v2 does not support Arabic script.

### Cited Findings
- ML Kit Text Recognition v2 supports the Chinese, Devanagari, Japanese, Korean and Latin scripts. Arabic is not supported — [Google ML Kit docs](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/text-recognition/v2)
- **Competitor survey** (marketing copy from store listings, unverified):
  - "Document Expiry Reminder" (iOS) is fully offline, uses Apple Vision on-device OCR, lists Arabic as a UI language and has a Family tier at $69.99 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6756968613)
  - "IdVault" (iOS) uses offline ML Kit OCR and sends alerts at 30, 7 and 0 days, with no Arabic or family features stated — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/th/app/idvault/id6758731177)
  - "DocHarbor" (iOS) is family-oriented, with cascading reminders. Its free tier allows reminders on only 2 documents — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6786043565)
  - "Travel Document Vault" (Android/iOS) has family profiles, offline storage and reminders at 6 months, 3 months, 6 weeks, 2 weeks and 1 week. No OCR is stated — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/travel-document-vault)
  - "DocStow" (Android) has family profiles and reminders but emphasises "Secure Cloud Access" — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.docstow.app)
  - "Proof Pocket" is offline with expiry reminders, with no family feature stated — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.proofpocket)

### Inferences
Gaps ranked by fit with Doc Keeper and by evidence of need:
1. **Arabic and RTL OCR plus a bilingual UI.** ML Kit has no Arabic support, so Android competitors using ML Kit cannot search Arabic text on Emirates IDs, Iqamas, Ejari contracts or Arabic certificates. Options are Tesseract's Arabic model on-device, or recognising Arabic fields from layout or MRZ. This is a defensible differentiator for the Gulf, though Arabic Tesseract accuracy is unverified.
2. **Automatic extraction of expiry and ID fields.** For example, parsing the passport MRZ (Latin and machine-readable, so ML Kit works) and the dates on Emirates ID and Iqama cards. Most competitors only offer manual reminder entry.
3. **Renewal workflow, not just reminders.** Model dependencies (residence visa, then Emirates ID, then labour card, then dependants' visas), the 30-day grace window and fine accrual, and a checklist of documents to prepare (photo, passport validity of 6 months or more, medical, insurance). No surveyed app offers this; the dependency is documented on [u.ae](https://u.ae/en/archived-content/residence-visa/renewing-your-residence-visa).
4. **Family sharing without a cloud.** Sync over the local network or Wi-Fi Direct, or an encrypted export via QR or file, between spouses' phones. Surveyed family apps either rely on the cloud or are single-device.
5. **iOS availability.** Many competitors are iOS-first; Doc Keeper is Android-only. Mixed-platform couples are a likely blocker for family features.
6. **Emergency access and digital legacy.** A sealed export or recovery-key handoff to a trusted contact. I found no competitor offering it offline.
7. **Cross-border bundles.** Pre-made "move to UAE", "travel pack" and "exit country" checklists.

### Gaps
- I found no published accuracy figures for Arabic OCR on-device (Tesseract or others) on ID cards.
- The competitor scan is from store-listing snippets only. I did not check feature depth, ratings or install counts.
- I found no survey data on how much users value offline-only storage compared with cloud backup.

## 4. What blocks distribution and trust?

### Takeaway
Distributing only as an APK from GitHub is becoming a serious problem. Google's developer verification took effect on 30 September 2026 in initial regions and expands globally in 2027. Unregistered apps will then need ADB or a one-time "advanced flow" with a 24-hour wait, which is prohibitive for mainstream Gulf users. The fix is to register as a verified developer. That works even for apps distributed outside Play. Joining Play as a new personal account adds a closed test with 12 testers for 14 days. Privacy-first peers built trust through open source, independent audits and transparent business models.

### Cited Findings
**Android developer verification**
- Verification opened to all developers on 30 March 2026. Limited-distribution accounts for students and hobbyists need only an email, cap installs at 20 devices, and went into early access in June 2026 with a global launch in August 2026. From 30 September 2026, "apps must be registered by verified developers in order to be installed and updated" on certified devices in initial regions. The original list was Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand; a later update says only "select regions". Expansion to all regions follows in 2027 — [Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/android-developer-verification-rolling-out-to-all-developers.html); [Developer verification guide](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides)
- Unregistered apps can still be installed with ADB or the "advanced flow". Enterprise apps on managed devices are exempt — [Google support: Understanding Android developer verification](https://support.google.com/android-developer-console/answer/16561738?hl=en)
- The advanced flow began rolling out in August 2026. The steps are: enable Developer Mode, toggle "Allow Unverified Packages", authenticate, restart and wait 24 hours. Users can then allow unverified apps for 7 days or indefinitely. ADB installs skip the wait — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/18/google-gradually-rolling-out-androids-advanced-sideloading-ahead-of-developer-verification/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-android-sideloading-unverified-apps-new-rules-3650343); [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/03/google-adds-24-hour-wait-for-unverified.html?hl=en)
- Play Console lets developers register apps distributed outside Play. Verified Play developers have eligible apps registered automatically — [Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/android-developer-verification-rolling-out-to-all-developers.html)

**Google Play onboarding**
- Personal developer accounts created after 13 November 2023 must run a closed test with at least 12 testers, opted in continuously for 14 days, before applying for production access — [Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en)
- Organisation accounts are reportedly exempt but need a D-U-N-S number, which can take up to 30 days. Passing the 14 days does not guarantee approval, and drops in tester engagement are reported to cause rejection. These are secondary sources — [Choicely](https://www.choicely.com/blog/google-play-12-tester-rule); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-play-app-testing-requirement-3510580)

**How privacy-first apps built trust**
- Standard Notes is open source and has had multiple independent audits. It joined Proton AG on 10 April 2024, and Proton committed to keep it open source and supported, citing its earlier stewardship of SimpleLogin — [Proton blog](https://proton.me/blog/da/proton-standard-notes-join-forces); [Privacy Guides](https://www.privacyguides.org/software/notebooks/)
- Obsidian is closed source but described as independently audited, with no telemetry and local-first files — [Proton comparison](https://proton.me/learn/european-alternatives/european-note-taking-app); [Kaspersky](https://www.kaspersky.com/blog/top-encrypted-todo-and-note-taking-apps/50915)
- Proton argues that open-source encryption lets researchers verify claims "instead of relying entirely on company promises". Proton is a competitor-adjacent source — [Proton](https://proton.me/learn/european-alternatives/european-note-taking-app)
- Listings by Privacy Guides and similar communities act as trust signals and discovery channels for audited, open-source tools — [Privacy Guides notebooks](https://privacyguides.org/en/notebooks)

### Inferences
- **Priority 1:** register as a verified developer now, and register the GitHub APK's signing key, before enforcement reaches the UAE (by 2027 at the latest). Otherwise every new user faces a 24-hour developer-mode flow. The UAE was not in the initial region list.
- A sideloaded vault for IDs carries a trust paradox: users are asked to bypass security warnings to install an app that guards their most sensitive documents. Play listing (or F-Droid or another verified store), reproducible builds, a public security write-up and ideally a third-party audit would ease this.
- The 12-tester rule is a modest hurdle for a solo developer and is achievable through community expat groups. A D-U-N-S-backed organisation account avoids it and adds credibility.
- Trust playbook taken from peers: open-source the crypto layer or the whole app, publish an audit, make an explicit no-network claim (for example, no INTERNET permission, which is verifiable in the manifest), use a one-time price with no data monetisation, and seek listings on Privacy Guides or similar.
- In the Gulf, distribution channels likely include expat community groups (Facebook, WhatsApp, Reddit r/dubai and r/UAE), Gulf News and Khaleej Times "how-to" content, and partnerships with PRO and visa-service firms. This is inference only and unverified.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Google Play policy pages for document and ID-storage apps, for example restrictions on the All Files Access (MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE) permission or rules for apps that store government ID images. These need checking before a Play submission.
- I found no confirmation of when the UAE or other GCC countries enter verification enforcement, beyond "2027 globally".
- I found no data on user drop-off rates for sideloaded installs, or on how much an audit affects conversion.
- I found no growth or revenue figures for Standard Notes, Proton or Obsidian that would quantify the trust strategy.
