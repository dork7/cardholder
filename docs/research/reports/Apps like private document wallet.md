# Rivals Match the Vault, Not the Answers

Yes, apps like this one exist, but only in halves. As of November 2026 at least seven offline, no-account document vaults ship on iOS or Android, and four of them already pair on-device OCR with expiry reminders; separately, a long tail of small apps runs "chat with your documents" on the phone. **No product found in this research joins the two halves**: an encrypted, account-free wallet for IDs, cards and PDFs on both Android and iOS, with on-device question answering that shows an answer only when a line of the document backs it. The closest vault (Travel Document Vault) has no AI, no tags, no widget and a travel-only scope; the closest AI app (Sigmy) is iOS-only, bundles a 3.4 GB model and is not a wallet. Tags, pinned cards and a home-screen widget are the rarest storage features, and Android is far thinner than iOS. Offline storage, no account and OCR of the expiry date are table stakes in this niche and differentiate nothing on their own. These conclusions rest on weak evidence: Google Play listings and reviews were largely unreadable, most claims are vendor copy or search snippets, several named products were never verified, and no market-size data was found, so "none found" means none found here, not none exists.

## Seven offline vaults cover six to nine of eleven storage features

The storage half of the concept is a crowded niche of single-developer apps launched in 2025 and 2026. Measured against eleven non-AI features (offline-first, on-device encryption, on-device OCR, suggested fields, expiry reminders, folders/tags/pins, a full-screen show mode, PDF export, a passphrase-encrypted backup file, a widget, and no analytics or account), the best match covers nine and none covers all eleven.

**Travel Document Vault is the nearest competitor and the only one of the four OCR-plus-reminder apps on Android.** It stores documents on the device with no account, encrypts files on disk, reads passport MRZ and expiry dates on-device, sends stepped reminders starting eight months before a passport expires, offers a protected "Show to Another Person" mode, exports single-document PDFs, and exports a password-protected `.tdvault` backup file for free ([TDV FAQ](https://traveldocumentvault.com/faq/), [TDV Features](https://traveldocumentvault.com/features/)). It mentions no folders, tags, pins or widget, its scope is travel documents, and its free tier stops at five documents; the Pro price of about $9.99 one-time comes from a search summary of [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/travel-document-vault), not the vendor.

On iOS, **Document Expiry Reminder** (developer MO NOUR) is the nearest match on privacy architecture: AES-256-GCM encryption of documents and attachments, Apple Vision OCR of expiry dates, encrypted backup files, "No accounts, no sync, no servers", and a "Data Not Collected" label, at $39.99 lifetime ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6756968613)). **DocHarbor** is the nearest match on organisation and intelligence: Apple Vision suggests title, category, owner and dates for the user to confirm, and it is the only vault found advertising both folders and widgets. It relies on iOS Data Protection instead of its own encryption, exports a plain zip, and declares analytics usage data ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6786043565)). **Docarus** adds NFC chip reading but puts reminders and encrypted backups behind Pro and limits the free plan to two documents ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6761782909)).

Three patterns matter more than any single row. First, **no app read mentions tags or pinned cards**, and only DocHarbor advertises a widget for documents. Second, **ID vaults and loyalty-card wallets are two separate families**. Catima is offline, open source and has a list widget and full-screen card view, but it is a barcode wallet with no encryption claim and no document storage ([F-Droid](https://f-droid.org/en/packages/me.hackerchick.catima/), [GitHub](https://github.com/CatimaLoyalty/Android)); Pass2U is ad-supported with Facebook, AdMob and Mixpanel SDKs detected across roughly 5.6M Android installs ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.passesalliance.wallet)); Stocard was folded into the Klarna app ([MacRumors forum](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/stocard-replacement.2434874/)). Folio is the only sizeable app spanning IDs and cards (4.7 from 6.9K US ratings), and it requires an account for backup, declares identifiers used for tracking, and runs selfie verification that reviewers resented ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1266382717), [Folio privacy policy](https://folio.id/en/privacy/)). Third, **the closest matches have no visible users**: every Tier A vault shows zero or one rating, so there is no review evidence of what users of an offline OCR vault like or dislike. Ente Locker brings a Cure53-audited end-to-end encrypted vault, but it is cloud-synced, and no source mentions OCR or reminders ([F-Droid](https://f-droid.org/en/packages/io.ente.locker.fdroid/), [Ente help](https://ente.com/help/locker/)).

No vault page read mentions generative AI, an LLM or an ask feature. "Smart" in this category tops out at on-device field recognition.

## On-device document chat ships on phones, outside any wallet

The AI half exists as its own fragmented category: small indie apps, mostly iOS, mostly launched in 2025 and 2026, plus open-source Android RAG projects. **Sigmy is the closest analogue to the concept's answer-with-source behaviour.** It runs a Google Gemma model locally, reads PDFs, Office files and images through on-device OCR, and states that "every answer cites a tappable source passage", with a "does not collect any data" label and pricing of $5.99 a month, $39.99 a year or $79.99 lifetime. It is also a 3.5 GB download because the model is bundled, it runs on iPhone and iPad only, and it shows no rating ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6762498646)). QuickIn Doc takes the opposite route, running "100% on-device with Apple Intelligence" for about $6.99 a year, which restricts it to iPhone 15 Pro and later and to iOS 26 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6755641190)). Off Grid, open source on Android and iOS, indexes PDFs locally and names the document an answer came from; its own team says the on-phone 2B model "handles simple lookups and short summaries well" and hands harder work to a larger model on the user's own network ([dev.to](https://dev.to/alichherawalla/how-to-build-a-private-knowledge-base-on-your-phone-with-local-ai-no-cloud-no-api-keys-5d2c)).

Three parts of the concept's AI design have no match among the apps found. **No app tiers "built-in model first, small downloaded model otherwise"**: each picks Apple Intelligence only, a bundled open model only, or local with a cloud or LAN escape hatch. **No app advertises suppressing an answer when no line supports it**; citation is claimed by a minority and a grounding check by none. And **no app puts the feature inside an encrypted ID and card wallet on both platforms**. A handful of new iOS apps market "vault plus scanner plus AI" (VaultScan AI, ScanOnce, DocuIntelli AI, PaperAI), but they surfaced only as search snippets, several do not say whether the model runs on the device, and none has reviews ([VaultScan AI](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6762499332), [ScanOnce](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/scanonce/id6759916701)). These are the most likely place for an unseen direct competitor, and they were not read in full.

The mainstream alternatives run in the cloud. Adobe states that "the use of generative AI features in Acrobat involves cloud processing of documents" ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/acrobat/using/acrobat-gen-ai-usage-policy.html)), and Private LLM's FAQ says it "does not currently support reading documents or files" ([Private LLM](https://privatellm.app/en/faq)).

The platform constraints explain why the tiered design is necessary, not ornamental. Apple's on-device model has "a context window of 4096 tokens per language model session", and overflow is a hard error ([Apple TN3193](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/technotes/tn3193-managing-the-on-device-foundation-model-s-context-window.md)). Gemini Nano through ML Kit runs only on a list of recent flagship-class devices, only while the app is in the foreground, and under a per-app quota ([ML Kit GenAI](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai)). Even Nutrient's commercial PDF SDK, which answers questions with Apple's model, lists no multi-document questions and no navigation to the passage where it found the answer, and warns it "may fail to find information that exists in the document" ([Nutrient](https://www.nutrient.io/guides/ios/ai-assistant/on-device.md)). One practitioner running a 1B model in an Android app reported fluent answers that drew false conclusions from correct data, fixed only by checking output with deterministic rules ([daily.dev summary](https://daily.dev/posts/why-a-1b-on-device-llm-needs-a-harness-xa1gvet8e)). The concept's line-backed answer rule is the same class of mitigation, and it is the part competitors do not advertise. No measured accuracy figures for document Q&A on phones were found, so the quality of any of these apps, including this one, is unproven by this research.

## Adjacent categories each solve two or three pieces, behind accounts

The realistic "I already use something for this" answer is a password manager. 1Password's Watchtower flags driver's licences and memberships expiring within two months and passports within ten ([1Password Support](https://support.1password.com/watchtower/)); NordPass and Dashlane also offer document items with expiry reminders, per search summaries of their support pages ([NordPass](https://support.nordpass.com/hc/en-us/articles/36113158693393-How-to-add-and-manage-documents-in-NordPass), [Dashlane](https://support.dashlane.com/hc/articles/202699401)). All of the mainstream managers are account-based cloud services, and file attachments sit behind paid tiers: Proton Pass lists attachments as "Not included" on its free plan, and Bitwarden includes them from Premium at $19.80 a year ([Proton Pass](https://proton.me/pass/pricing), [Bitwarden](https://bitwarden.com/pricing/)). Enpass is the exception on storage, keeping the vault in the user's own cloud and never on its servers ([Enpass](https://www.enpass.io/pricing/)). None verified here reads the text of an arbitrary scan or answers questions about it.

| Life-admin organisers | Family sharing, emergency access, AI answers at one tier | Cloud and account by design; high price | $100 to $480 per year |

The "covers" and "missing" columns are synthesis across the notes, not vendor statements. Price points: [Bitwarden](https://bitwarden.com/pricing/), [Genius Scan](https://thegrizzlylabs.com/genius-scan/pricing), [Expiro](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6758278098), [RenewalKit](https://apps.apple.com/pg/app/id6758590671), [Trustworthy](https://www.trustworthy.com/pricing), [Everplans](https://www.everplans.com/pricing).

Scanner apps end at a clean searchable PDF. Genius Scan processes and stores documents on the phone "unless you explicitly decide to export them", with OCR in its $39.99-a-year tier ([Genius Scan](https://thegrizzlylabs.com/genius-scan/pricing)), and no scanner read detects an expiry date or schedules a reminder. Microsoft retired Lens in early 2026 and points users to OneDrive, which "does not support saving scans locally on your device" ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/lens/)), removing the main free offline scanner from both stores. Dedicated expiry trackers form a second long tail in which offline, no-account operation and on-device date OCR are standard, yet most make no encryption claim for stored images and some store no images at all ([Expiro](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6758278098), [RenewalKit](https://apps.apple.com/pg/app/id6758590671), [Document Expiration RemindMe](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6478255369)). Life-admin services sit at the far end: Trustworthy's Gold tier lists "AI answers" at $20 a month billed annually, the closest adjacent equivalent to asking questions of one's documents, in a cloud account ([Trustworthy](https://www.trustworthy.com/pricing)).

Pricing norms follow from this map. The indie vaults and trackers cluster at a small free tier of two to five documents plus a lifetime unlock between $10 and $60; a price near password-manager levels invites the comparison to a product many users already pay for.

## Platform and government wallets hold issued credentials, not private papers

Apple, Google and Samsung wallets grew in 2025 and 2026 by adding issuers, not by widening what a user stores. Apple Wallet holds state IDs in 16 US states plus Puerto Rico and a US-passport Digital ID accepted only at select TSA checkpoints for domestic travel ([Apple](https://learn.wallet.apple/id)). Google Wallet's "Everything else" is the nearest platform feature to this app: it turns a photograph of a single card or pass into a Wallet item, tied to a Google account, with unevenly documented country coverage ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-wallet-everything-else-pass-rolling-out-3593890/)). The only shipped "ask about my wallet" feature is Gemini's connection to Google Wallet, which a search snippet describes as opt-in, US-only and covering passes such as boarding passes and loyalty cards ([TalkAndroid](https://www.talkandroid.com/532195-gemini-now-pulls-google-wallet-passes-and-spending-into-one-view/)). None of the three stores contracts, policies or multi-page PDFs as wallet items, and no source showed any of them sending expiry reminders.

Government wallets are far larger and structurally different. UAE Pass has more than 12 million registered users and DigiLocker more than 724 million, per search snippets of official reporting ([WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/c1betiw-world-economic-forum-recognises-uae-pass-global), [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/digilocker-crosses-7243-crore-users-umang-records-1166-crore-registrations-govt-informs-rajya-sabha)). Each is pull-from-issuer: the user requests a credential from an authority that already holds the record, which makes the document verifiable and excludes anything the state did not issue, such as a home-country passport, a foreign insurance policy or a relative's papers. DigiLocker's roughly 1 GB self-upload drive is the documented exception, and uploads carry no legal validity ([Stable Money](https://stablemoney.in/blog/how-to-upload-documents-in-digilocker)). The EU Digital Identity Wallet is due by 24 December 2026 with most member states expected to be late or partial ([eID Easy](https://www.eideasy.com/blog/eu-digital-identity-wallets-september-2026)).

The relationship is complementary, and the app should not claim otherwise. A scan in a private wallet is a personal copy; platform and government wallets issue verifiable credentials accepted at checkpoints and counters. What the private wallet holds is everything those wallets omit, and it keeps it off servers, which is the asset class behind the IDScan.net incident, where a marketplace reportedly offered more than 153 million driver's licences in September 2026 ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/11/idscan-net-data-breach-153-million-drivers-licenses/)).

## What exists, what is absent, and how far this evidence reaches

Existing apps already cover, individually and in several cases together: offline storage with no account; app-level encryption on the device; on-device OCR of expiry dates and passport fields; suggested title, category and dates (DocHarbor); multi-step expiry reminders; a protected show mode and per-document PDF export (Travel Document Vault); password-protected backup files (Travel Document Vault, Proof Pocket, Keevan, LibreCrate, Document Expiry Reminder); a widget (DocHarbor for documents, Catima for barcode cards); and, in separate apps, on-device document Q&A with passage citation (Sigmy) or on the phone's built-in model (QuickIn Doc).

Nothing found covers the following. No app combines an encrypted document wallet with on-device question answering on Android; every vault-plus-AI candidate found is iOS-only and unverified. No app advertises a rule that withholds an answer unless a document line supports it. No app tiers the built-in platform model ahead of a small downloaded one. No vault read mentions tags or pinned cards, and no single offline app holds IDs, insurance documents, barcode membership cards and PDFs together with OCR suggestions, reminders and a widget. On Android specifically, only Travel Document Vault pairs no-account storage with OCR and reminders, and no open-source app covers the concept.

These absences are bounded by what could be read. **Google Play pages were truncated by the fetch tool**, so Android install counts, ratings, Data safety declarations and written reviews are missing for nearly every app, and the Android expiry trackers are known only from search summaries; the "Android is thin" finding is therefore the least secure in this report. **Most feature and privacy claims are vendor copy**: App Store privacy labels are developer-declared, no Tier A vault has an independent audit, and no AI app was independently tested. **Much of the AI and platform-wallet material is search snippets**, including the Gemini Wallet integration, the vault-plus-AI candidates and several prices, some of which conflict between sources. **Several named products were never verified**, among them Trunk, Cards - Mobile Wallet, WalletPasses, Card Diary, Documents by Readdle, Notion and Evernote as improvised stores, Apple Passwords and Google Password Manager, NotebookLM mobile, Samsung Galaxy AI document features, and Nok Nok and LifeFile; they are unknown, not absent. No Reddit threads surfaced. **No market-size, download or revenue data was found** beyond one unsourced aggregator, and no survey measures willingness to pay for a private document wallet.

## Conclusion

The competitive question has shifted from "does anyone do this" to "which half is defensible". The storage half is a commodity: a newcomer listing "offline, AES-256, Face ID, expiry reminders" reads identically to a dozen unrated apps, and the indie pricing floor of a five-document free tier and a cheap lifetime unlock is already set. The differentiators that survive this research are breadth (IDs, policies, barcode cards and PDFs in one wallet with real organisation), parity on Android where the field is thinnest, and a grounded on-device answer feature that competitors would need months of harness work to copy, since the hard part is the verification layer and not the model. Sigmy and the unread vault-plus-AI apps show the direction of travel; the window in which this combination is unoccupied is probably short.

The strongest next step is to close the evidence gap, not widen the feature list: read the Google Play listings by hand for Travel Document Vault, Proof Pocket, Docsly and the expiry trackers, install VaultScan AI, ScanOnce and Sigmy to test what their AI does and where it runs, and treat any uniqueness claim in store copy as provisional until that is done. The absence of reviews for every close competitor also means nobody has yet demonstrated demand for an offline OCR vault at scale, so the same data that shows little competition shows no proven market.
